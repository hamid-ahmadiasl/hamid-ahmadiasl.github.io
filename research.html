  • Abstract

    Machine-learning surrogates are widely used to reduce the cost of computational fluid dynamics (CFD), but reliable performance can be difficult to establish when only a small and non-uniformly sampled design set is available. This study examines data quality and generalization reliability using 33 CFD configurations generated for a Savonius wind-turbine deflector study. The results show that small-sample surrogate reliability depends jointly on data quantity, target learnability, DOE coverage, corruption sensitivity, and uncertainty calibration, not on cross-validation accuracy alone.

  • Abstract

    External deflectors can improve flow around Savonius turbines, but their effects depend on interacting geometric parameters. This study combines two-dimensional steady-state CFD with design of experiments to evaluate deflector number, installation angle, and normalized deflector length using local driving-side velocity as the primary response. A 16-run full-factorial design was followed by 17 supplementary CFD configurations for response-surface refinement. Deflector number was statistically significant (p = 0.028), while length, angle, and the Length × Angle interaction showed weaker evidence. The highest directly simulated driving-side velocity was 5.015 m/s for five deflectors, a normalized deflector length of 2, and an angle of 45°. The quadratic response-surface model showed strong curvature and a significant Length × Angle interaction, but overpredicted an existing CFD case by 23.2%. The response surface is therefore interpreted as a tool for characterizing nonlinear trends, not as a validated optimum. The conclusions are limited to local flow responses.

  • Abstract

    Machine-learning reconstruction can reduce the number of measurements required to characterize experimental flow fields, but its reliability depends on the quality and spatial structure of the available data. This study quantifies several data-quality effects using velocity measurements from an open-circuit blower wind tunnel. Two tunnel configurations were measured at 968 spatial locations across eight cross-sections in four temporally separated full-tunnel campaigns. Five regression models were compared under repeated spatial holdout, with selected models then used for independent-campaign and leave-one-section-out validation. Histogram Gradient Boosting achieved a baseline RMSE of 0.109 m/s (R² = 0.920). Averaging three measurement campaigns instead of one reduced the RMSE for prediction of an independent campaign from 0.247 to 0.217 m/s. At a budget of 64 points per cross-section, a regular space-filling layout reduced KNN reconstruction error by 37% relative to the mean random layout. With the missing fraction fixed at 20.7%, contiguous 5 × 5 missing regions produced 55% higher mean error than randomly missing points. Strict section-level reconstruction error also increased strongly with a spatial gradient index of flow complexity (Spearman ρ = 0.929 for KNN and 0.862 for Histogram Gradient Boosting). Small bounded perturbations had limited influence, whereas severe noise and outlier contamination caused substantial degradation; a robust absolute-error loss recovered part of the outlier-induced loss. The results show that reconstruction reliability is governed not only by data quantity, but also by measurement repetition, placement, missing-data topology, and physical field complexity.

  • Abstract

    Achieving uniform airflow in open-circuit blower wind tunnels requires conventional flow-conditioning components such as contraction sections, honeycombs, multiple screens, and settling chambers. Although effective, these components increase tunnel length, complexity, and pressure losses. This study evaluates a simplified flow-conditioning strategy for a constant-area blower wind tunnel using slanting guide walls, vibrating wall-mounted baffles, and a single screen.

    The original and modified configurations were evaluated under identical operating conditions through four measurement campaigns, each covering 968 sampling locations across eight tunnel sections. Flow quality was assessed from cross-sectional velocity distributions, the coefficient of variation (CoV), and the Velocity Difference Ratio (VDR), used as a study-specific normalized temporal velocity-range metric.

    The modified configuration produced more uniform airflow with a smaller measured temporal velocity range, particularly in upstream and intermediate sections. The CoV decreased by an average of 28.6%, with a maximum reduction of 43.8% at Section 5. The section-averaged VDR decreased by 22.3%, with the largest reduction of 28.2% at Section 6. Comparable flow uniformity was observed approximately 2 m closer to the tunnel inlet than in the original configuration. The overall cross-sectional mean velocity decreased by only 1.2%, indicating no substantial loss of airflow.

    These results show that a simplified conditioning arrangement can accelerate flow development and improve both spatial uniformity and temporal stability without requiring a conventional contraction section or complete honeycomb-screen system. The proposed approach offers a practical option for large laboratory-scale blower wind tunnels where space and manufacturing complexity are design constraints.

  • Abstract

    Over the recent decades, many different ducted turbines have been designed to augment efficiency of wind turbines. The design and number of blades are the most important parameters to optimize efficiency of wind turbines.

    In this paper, effects of design, number and attack angles of blades on rotational speed are experimentally studied in a duct which increases wind velocity up to 2.46 times numerically and 2.32 times experimentally as great as far-field flow. In order to realize this, 3 different types of aerodynamic blades were designed and then, 2-bladed, 3-bladed and 4-bladed impellers were created by these blades; finally, 9 impellers were built on aggregate. The rotational speed of each impeller was recorded at 7 attack angles in two operating states with and without the duct at the same conditions in a wind tunnel. Results demonstrate that firstly, the rotational speed is reduced by adding more blades; secondly, the impellers with wide top blades have lower rotational speeds; thirdly, the rotational speed is augmented by increasing attack angle from 0 to 75°; therefore, the rotational speed increase ratio is dependent on these 3 parameters and the wind velocity increase ratio at the throat as well.

  • Abstract

    Existence of coastal regions in Iran has made this country a potential area to produce hydro-electric. Ducted or shrouded turbines generate more output power by increasing fluid velocity; therefore, they turn potential areas to actual areas to produce electricity. The purpose of this study is to investigate effects of a shroud with airfoil cross-section form on upwind and downwind streams which is a vital parameter of performance and usage of this kind of turbines. To do this, the fluid velocity was measured at eight sections behind and in front of the shroud in total and the velocity profiles were compared with numerical simulation results. The results showed that the inconsiderable increase of pressure in front and fast disappearance of the effects of shroud behind it, which is as a result of specific streamed-line design, are some advantages that cause to install more shrouds in a defined area.

  • Abstract

    Existence of the coasts in the north and south of Iran cause to create potential areas to produce electricity by tidal and wave energies. Therefore, increase of fluid velocity is required to exploit these areas. A new generation of turbines is created by designing and building a shroud to increase fluid velocity which it increases kinetic energy of flow and generates more electricity at coastlines and riverbeds. Based on airfoils characteristics, a shroud with an airfoil cross-section form was designed which increases water velocity approximately 2.8 times greater than far field flow numerically. The shroud was built and tested experimentally. Moreover, several propellers were installed in the shroud. Rotational speeds of the propellers in two states of with and without shroud were compared and they were proportional to increase ratio of flow velocity. Consequently, more power is generated by installing a turbine in the shroud.
